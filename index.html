How to convert annual salary to hourly rate

The basic annual salary to hourly rate formula is straightforward: divide your annual gross salary by the number of hours you work in a year.

Hourly rate = Annual salary ÷ (Hours per week × Weeks per year)

Example: $80,000 ÷ (38 × 52) = approximately $40.49 per hour before tax.

If you work a different number of ordinary hours, change the hours-per-week field. If your calculation excludes unpaid periods, change the weeks-per-year field too. This makes the result more useful than assuming every worker has the same schedule.

Why the calculator starts at 38 hours

Fair Work states that the National Employment Standards set a 38-hour weekly reference for full-time employees, with reasonable additional hours possible. Awards, enterprise agreements and individual arrangements can affect actual ordinary hours.

Source: Fair Work Ombudsman — Maximum weekly hours.

Hourly to annual salary formula

To work in the other direction, multiply your hourly rate by your weekly hours and the number of weeks you want included in the year.

Annual salary = Hourly rate × Hours per week × Weeks per year

Example: $40/hour × 38 hours × 52 weeks = $79,040 per year before tax.

Salary and hourly rate FAQs

How do I convert an annual salary to an hourly rate in Australia?

Divide the annual salary by total annual work hours. With 38 hours per week and 52 weeks, annual work hours are 1,976. For example, $80,000 ÷ 1,976 is about $40.49 per hour before tax.

How much is $80,000 a year per hour in Australia?

At 38 hours per week for 52 weeks, $80,000 per year is approximately $40.49 per hour before tax.

How much is $100,000 a year per hour?

At 38 hours per week for 52 weeks, $100,000 per year is approximately $50.61 per hour before tax.

What is the full-time weekly hours reference in Australia?

Fair Work’s National Employment Standards use 38 hours per week as the maximum weekly hours reference for full-time employees, unless reasonable additional hours apply. Actual ordinary hours can vary under an award, agreement or employment arrangement.

Does this salary calculator include tax or superannuation?

No. It is a gross-pay conversion calculator. It does not calculate income tax, superannuation, overtime, penalty rates, allowances or leave loading.

Why can my actual hourly value differ from the result?

Your employment package may include paid leave, superannuation, bonuses, allowances, overtime or other entitlements. Your contract or industrial instrument may also define hours differently. Use this tool for gross conversion, not as a payroll or legal-entitlement calculation.
